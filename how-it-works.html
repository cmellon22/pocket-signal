BEHIND THE SIGNAL

How Pocket Signal works

Pocket Signal combines multiple predictive models to turn NFL data into easy-to-read game forecasts and player outlooks.

THE IDEA

One matchup. Multiple perspectives.

Instead of asking one model to predict everything, Pocket Signal uses separate models for different parts of the game.

The result is a forecast that shows who is more likely to win, the expected scoring margin, and the expected total points.

CONFIDENCE

Signal strength

Signal strength represents confidence in the winner forecast — not a guarantee of the result.

Strong 70%+

The winner model sees a larger separation between the two teams.

Medium 60–69.9%

Pocket Signal has a clear preference, but there is still meaningful uncertainty.

Weak Below 60%

The matchup is closer and the winner forecast is more uncertain.

MODEL DISAGREEMENT

What happens when the models disagree?

The winner and margin forecasts are produced by separate models, so close games can occasionally produce different answers.

Pocket Signal does not force the models to agree. The disagreement stays visible so users can see the additional uncertainty in the matchup.

MODEL DISAGREEMENT
Winner model NYG 60.5%
Margin model NO by 1.3

Historically, the winner model has been slightly more accurate at selecting the outright winner, but the difference is small.

THE FORECAST

What could their week look like?

Quarterbacks have passing-yard, attempt and completion projections. Receivers and tight ends have receiving-yard, reception and target projections. Running backs have rushing-yard, receiving-yard and reception projections.

These are estimates, not exact outcomes. Consider other forecasts, injury updates and matchup information when forming your own outlook.

RECENT PERFORMANCE

Put the projection in context.

Blue chart bars show recorded game results; cyan shows the selected week's projection. Season and recent averages help you compare the forecast with the player's usual production.

Where available, the gold line marks the market benchmark. A player's past results against that line describe their history, not their probability of going over this week.

MATCHUP OUTLOOK

Look beyond the player.

Compare what the opponent has allowed to the player's position across the season and its last three completed games. These figures combine production from all players at that position, not one individual.

The game environment adds projected team points, margin and total. These are supporting comparisons, not a breakdown of how the player model calculated its forecast.

MAKE IT YOUR BOARD

Find the players that matter to you.

Search by player or team, filter by position or game, and use the stars to build a watchlist in your browser. Sort by market gap to bring the largest projection-to-line differences to the top.

Availability and starting roles can change. Check current reports before relying on an outlook; a missing report does not confirm that a player is active.

PLAYER MARKET GAPS

Where our forecast differs from the line.

The gap is Pocket Signal's projection minus the saved sportsbook line. Green means the projection is above the line; red means it is below.

Sorting by gap size uses the absolute difference, so a large negative gap can appear beside a large positive one. Across all positions, the main yardage stat is QB passing, RB rushing, or WR/TE receiving.

A larger gap is a larger disagreement, not a win probability or a guarantee. Players without a matching line appear last and show “No market line available yet.” Lines are snapshots and may have moved.

Hit rate excludes pending results, pushes and voids. Hypothetical units use the recorded odds and one unit risked per selection. Reconstructed archives are labeled separately from pregame snapshots.
